Research Interests

Metaphysics, Ethics, Epistemology.

Overview

My philosophical interests lie at the intersection of metaphysics and ethics, encompassing topics such as personal identity over time, moral status, the ontological status of collectives, and issues related to collective responsibility.

My thesis, The Worm View and the Personite Problem: Towards a Collectivist Solution, focuses on perdurantism — specifically the Worm View — and Mark Johnston's personite problem. I explore various potential perdurantist solutions to the problem, with a pronounced focus on a collectivist approach.

More broadly, I work on personal identity and persistence, four-dimensionalism and perdurantism, and the metaphysics of time, and on how different theories of persistence bear on questions in normative ethics, such as moral responsibility and desire-satisfaction. I also work on methodological questions in metaphysics, particularly on how metaphysicians should choose between rival theories, including under normative or theistic pressure.

My PhD is supported by the South, West, and Wales Doctoral Training Partnership (AHRC).
